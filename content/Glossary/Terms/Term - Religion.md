# Religion

> [!abstract] Definition 
> A sophisticated system of rituals, symbols, and metaphors designed to help humanity navigate the existential realities of suffering, community, and transcendence. Rather than a mere collection of abstract dogmas, religion functions as an active, lived framework that bridges the gap between intellectual belief and physical action.

## 🎯 Criteria
- **Core Requirement:** To qualify as a religion, a system must provide an existential map addressing ultimate meaning, translate beliefs into daily moral choices through ethical frameworks, and reinforce social cohesion through repetitive physical rituals and communal habituation.
- **Exclusions:** It explicitly excludes purely abstract, disembodied dogmas devoid of ritual practice, as well as empirical claims about the material universe that attempt to substitute for scientific inquiry.

## ⚡ Examples
- **[[Christianity]]:** Christian traditions function as highly embodied frameworks, where sacraments, communal fasts, and liturgical rituals translate ultimate dogmas into daily, lived reality.
- **[[Buddhism]]:** Buddhism operates as a pragmatic existential map where repetitive somatic habits are used communally to actively navigate the reality of human suffering.
- **[[Ancient Greek Polytheism]]:** Far from a dead collection of colorful myths, this system operated as a deeply civic engine, demanding physical sacrifices and strict adherence to moral codes to maintain cosmic and social cohesion.
- **(*Non-Example*) Deism:** While offering a rational theory of a creator, Deism remains a purely intellectual, disembodied philosophy that lacks any communal rituals, active moral frameworks, or lived practices to help humanity navigate existential suffering.
