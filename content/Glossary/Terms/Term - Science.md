# Science

> [!abstract] Definition 
> **Science** is a systematic, empirical **testing methodology** used to investigate, model, and decode the mechanics of the physical universe. It operates strictly within the material domain through a continuous, repeatable loop of observation, hypothesis formation, experimentation, and data analysis.

## 🎯 Criteria

* **Core Requirement:** To qualify as science, an inquiry must deal exclusively with phenomena that are **measurable, repeatable, and falsifiable** within the material domain, which can be rigorously subjected to an empirical testing capable of proving it wrong.
* **Exclusions:** This term explicitly excludes any questions regarding **intrinsic purpose, aesthetics, or moral value**. Because it is a strictly value-neutral testing method, it can only measure *how* a physical process functions, completely separate from whether that process is ethically good, meaningful, or spiritually significant.

## ⚡ Examples
* **Galileo's Leaning Tower Experiment**: Galileo dropped two spheres of unequal weight from the Leaning Tower of Pisa; both hit the ground at the same time. This is a classic demonstration of **falsifiability**, proving that empirical testing can instantly shatter centuries of elegant but untested philosophical theories.
* **Newton's Prism Experiment**: Newton passed white sunlight through a glass prism to split it into a rainbow, then passed a single color through a second prism, which remained unchanged. This showcases the power of **isolating variables**, proving that color is an inherent property of light rather than a modification caused by the glass.
* **Franklin's Kite Experiment**: Franklin flew a kite with a metal key attached during a thunderstorm, drawing a spark to prove lightning is basic electricity. This demonstrates **empirical predictability**, where testing a physical hypothesis immediately yielded concrete, life-saving technological optimization (the lightning rod).
