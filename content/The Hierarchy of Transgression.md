# The Hierarchy of Transgression: Degrees of Sin in Scripture

A common misconception in modern theological discussion is that all sins are viewed as functionally identical in their consequences. However, a close reading of both the Old and New Testaments reveals a clear distinction: some sins are more severe than others.

## The Distinction of Intent
The foundational framework for this hierarchy is established in the Mosaic Law. When God spoke to Moses, He differentiated between **unintentional** and **defiant** (intentional) sins.

For the community that strays from God without intent, a path to restoration through offering is provided:
> [!quote] Numbers 15:26 (NIV)
> "The whole Israelite community and the foreigners residing among them will be forgiven, because all the people were involved in the unintentional wrong."

However, the "High Handed" or intentional sin carries a much weightier judgment. Because the individual has "despised the Lord’s word," the consequence is total separation from the community [^1]. This distinction proves that different sins carry different punishments and are valued differently by the Divine Law.

## New Testament Continuance
This theme of "levels of sin" persists into the New Testament. We see it clearly in the Johannine epistles and the teachings of Christ:

*   **Johannine Distinction:** In 1 John 5, a separation is made between sins that "do not lead to death" and those that "lead to death" (v. 16-17).
*   **Comparative Judgment:** Jesus suggests a sliding scale of accountability, stating it would be "more bearable for Sodom" on judgment day than for cities like Chorazin and Bethsaida that witnessed His miracles but did not repent (Matthew 11:24).

Ultimately, the New Testament reinforces that while grace is available, the severity of a sin is often tied to the **knowledge** and **intent** of the sinner.
