Some sins are more sever than other one. This can be seen all over the Bible. In the Old Testament when God telling Moses that the community can commit unintentional sins and intentional sins. If the community commits an unintentional sins, they must do some offerings and then "the whole Israelite community and the foreigners residing among them will be forgiven, because all the people were involved in the unintentional wrong" ([[Numbers 15]]:26, NIV). However, for people committing intentional sins, then "Because they have despised the Lord’s word and broken his commands, they must surely be cut off; their guilt remains on them" (Numbers 15:31, NIV). This distinction shows that different sins have different punishments and are thus valued differently. Intentional sins are worse and could not be forgiven. 

This distinction can be seen through out the New Testament too. In [[1 John 5]], John mentions that there are sins that do "not lead to death" (v. 16, NIV) and sins that do "\[lead\] to death" (v.17, NIV), separating two types of sins. Jesus even states that "it will be more bearable for Sodom on the day of judgment than for \[cities of Chorazin and Bethsaida\]" ([[Matthew 11]]:24, NIV).  Even in the New Testament their is several scenarios where sins have different levels of consequences.
