> [!abstract] Overview 
> A central tension exists in theology: is sin a single, binary state of separation, or a graduated scale of severity? By combining the concepts of **[[The Consequence of Sin - The Fundamental Separation|The Fundamental Separation]]** and the **[[Hierarchy of Transgression]]**, we can understand sin as both a universal breach of relationship and a progressive distancing from the Divine.

## The Two Competing Models

1. **The Fundamental Separation**: This view posits that any sin, regardless of its nature, separates man from God. While it underscores the universal need for grace, it carries the risk of moral nihilism—the "all sins are the same" trap that can lead to justifying greater evils because "forgiveness is always available".
    
2. **The Hierarchy of Transgression**: Supported by scriptural distinctions between "unintentional" and "defiant" sins, this model recognizes that some acts carry more weight than others. However, this can lead to the "lesser sin" trap, where smaller transgressions are excused as being "not that bad" in comparison.
    
## The Integrated Solution:

To avoid these traps, we must recognize that while every sin incurs an initial "cost" of guilt and separation, the distance of that separation increases with the severity of the act. This balanced approach is reflected in the works of key figures in the "Internal Forum":

- **[[Augustine|St. Augustine]]**: He noted that while "crime is grievous sin" and deserves the highest condemnation, God "approves of none" and "hates them all"[^1]. This establishes that all sin is a rejection of God, even while some are uniquely heinous.
    
- **[[Thomas Aquinas|St. Thomas Aquinas]]**: He described sin as a "sickness of the soul"[^2]. He further distinguished between **Mortal Sins** (irreparable defects that corrupt the fundamental principle of the soul)[^3] and **Venial Sins** (disorders that weaken the relationship without breaking it)[^4].
    
## The Framework of Responsibility

According to the **[[Council of Trent]]**, a sin reaches the level of "Mortal" separation when three criteria are met:

1. **Grave Matter**: The act itself is a serious violation.
    
2. **Full Knowledge**: The individual understands the act is wrong.
    
3. **Deliberate Consent**: The act is committed with full intent[^5].
    

By emphasizing that all sin should be avoided while reserving the greatest gravity for the most severe transgressions, we maintain both moral urgency and a realistic hierarchy of value.
