## Types of Governments
- [ ] _[[The Politics]]_ by [[Aristotle]]
		Defines the 3 core government types: Monarchy, Oligarchy, and Democracy
- [ ] _[[The Leviathan]]_ by [[Thomas Hobbes]]
		Strong case for monarchy using the ideas of human nature
- [ ] _[[The Spirit of the Laws]]_ by Montesquieu
		Strong influence on founding fathers. Classifies governments into Republics, Monarchies, and Despotisms, mapping each to an underlying principle
- [ ] _[[Competitive Authoritarianism: Hybrid Regimes After the Cold War]]_ by [[Steven Levitsky]] and [[Lucan A. Way]]
		defining modern types of governance beyond classical categories.
- [ ] _[[Second Treatise of Government]]_ by [[John Locke]]
- [ ] _[[The Prince]]_ by [[Niccolò Machiavelli]]
		Arguments for Monarchy while also talking about morals of government leaders
- [ ] [[The Social Contract]] by [[Rousseau]]





## Functions of Government
- [ ] _[[The Origins of Political Order_ (and _Political Order and Political Decay)]]_ by [[Francis Fukuyama]]
		Traces political development across human history, identifying three pillars of a functional state: **state capacity**, **rule of law**, and **democratic accountability**. It analyzes why some societies achieve balance while others descend into patrimonialism or state failure.
- [ ] _[[Political Order in Changing Societies]]_ by [[Samuel P. Huntington]]
		A classic work on political stability, state capacity, and regime transition
- [ ] [[Secular Cycles]] by [[Peter Turchin]]
		Describes cycles of power that effect all governments/societies
- [ ] _The Neurosis of Psychology / Modern Man in Search of a Soul_ by Carl Jung
		In _The Undiscovered Self_, Jung directly addresses the relationship between individual self-awareness, state collectivism, and the danger of mass psychological movements overwhelming political stability.
- [ ] _The Clash of Civilizations and the Remaking of World Order_ by Samuel P. Huntington
		Huntington argues that post-Cold War conflicts are driven by deep-seated cultural and civilizational identities rather than political ideologies—a foundational premise in Lynch’s macro-historical breakdowns.





## Critiques of Governments
- [ ] _[[The Myth of the Rational Voter]]_ by [[Bryan Caplan]]
		Provides a modern political economy critique of democratic decision-making.
- [ ] _The Gulag Archipelago_ by Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn
		Solzhenitsyn details how totalizing ideologies and small individual moral compromises collapse political systems into tyranny and atrocities.




## US Constitution
- [ ] _[[The Federalist Papers]]_ by [[Alexander Hamilton]], [[James Madison]], and [[John Jay]]
		Explains the reasons for the constitution 
- [ ] _[[Democracy in America]]_ by [[Alexis de Tocqueville]]
		Details how democracy fosters equality and civic participation, but warns of "the tyranny of the majority"




## Unsorted
- [ ] _The Cave and the Light: Plato Versus Aristotle, and the Struggle for the Soul of Western Civilization_ by Arthur Herman
		Traces how Western liberty was built on the dual pillars of Judeo-Christian values (Jerusalem) and Greek rationalism (Athens), detailing the ongoing tension between utopian planning and practical realism.
- [ ] _Capitalism and Freedom_ by Milton Friedman
		A foundational defense of free-market capitalism, arguing that competitive economic freedom is both a necessary condition for and a virtue of political freedom.
- [ ] _The Constitution of Liberty_ by Friedrich Hayek
		A comprehensive treatise on classical liberalism, rule of law, and how freedom requires spontaneous order rather than central government planning.
