## The Golden Mean
Aristotle defines Virtue as being in-between deficiency and excess, which were considered vices. This is Aristotle's Golden Mean. The Golden Mean is useful in becoming virtuous which  you can achieve by applying hardwood and dedication to making. (Nicomachean Ethics)

"It is by taking part in transactions with our fellow men that some of us become fair-minded and others iniquitous; by acting in dangerous situations and forming a habit of fear or of confidence we become courageous or cowardly." 


It is about becoming the right thing.  

"at the right times, about the right things, towards the right people, for the right end, and in the right way, is the intermediate and best condition, and this is proper to virtue." (Nicomachean Ethics)

Will Durant puts it like "The golden mean… is not, like the mathematical mean, an exact average of two precisely calculable extremes; it fluctuates with the collateral circumstances of each situation, and discovers itself only to mature and flexible reason." (Story of Philosophy)

The goal then is to thus to push yourself to be virtuous. Virtue is learnable. You can attain with with practice. 



## The Principle of Polarity
Meanwhile the Hermetics think similar on a spectrum. To them _"Everything is Dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites..."_ (Kybalion). Instead of having to vices, Hermetics looked of having a positive and a negative pole. While you don't always want to be at the positive or negative pole, you can change the vibrations around you to move yourself up and down the spectrum. 

## Synthesis
Both the Golden Mean and the Principle of Polarity are great tools to measure the world. By using a spectrum, they both give degrees of flexibility. Aristotle talks more about being able to move actively move yourself on the spectrum while the Hermetics talk about how rhythm moves things like a pendulum on a spectrum. Although, you still can negate the pendulum swings to a degree. On this front, the Hermetics have a greater view of the spectrum as it incorporates the natural osculation of the other influences in the world where Aristotle's mean doesn't not mention the same flexibility. However, Aristotle allows the spectrum to be defined as two opposites that allow the user to move toa virtuous place in between, which is more nuanced then the Hermetics. When talking about moving yourself up and down the spectrum, Hermetics and Aristotle both agree that you have to transmute the things arounds (like habits) to make changes in your position on the spectrum. 


The goal is then to take action to change the things around you so you can swing from a vice to a virtue. 















## Outline
